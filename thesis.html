Title

Assisting the integration of privacy concerns into software: an MDE-based framework for GDPR-aware software engineering

Thesis manuscript — available after the defence Defence slides — available after the defence

Abstract

The generalisation of the digital transformation makes privacy protection a critical requirement of software systems. As organisations process ever larger volumes of personal data, complying with regulations such as the GDPR calls for disciplined, tool-supported engineering practices instead of ad-hoc ones. Existing approaches often fail to bridge the gap between legal requirements and concrete implementations, creating compliance vulnerabilities and operational inefficiencies.

This thesis introduces PRIAM (PRIvacy Assessment Method), a generic methodological framework based on model-driven engineering (MDE) for integrating privacy protection by design. It formalises GDPR requirements in a metamodel that maps regulatory obligations to software specifications. A domain-specific language derived from this metamodel generates artefacts tailored to the context of an application and directly integrable into existing systems during implementation. To further assist developers, PRIAM adopts a scalable microservice architecture, secured by an authentication and authorisation service, and provides a dynamic consent manager based on ABAC (Attribute-Based Access Control) so that user consent controls data access at any time.

The result is a lightweight and non-intrusive design approach that lets developers integrate GDPR support into applications that already exist or are being designed. A French résumé is included in the manuscript.

Context

Ubiquitous software systems now collect, store and process personal data at large scale, in sectors such as healthcare, finance, e-commerce, public administration and the Internet of Things. Beyond the benefits of personalised services, this exposes individuals to data leaks, unauthorised secondary use, profiling and surveillance, as shown by high-profile cases such as Cambridge Analytica and the Equifax breach. Technical countermeasures (cryptography, anonymisation, fine-grained access control) are essential but do not give users real control over their data. Regulations such as the GDPR have turned Privacy by Design from a voluntary good practice into a legal requirement, yet translating legal texts into engineering practice remains difficult.

Problem

Empirical studies show that developers struggle to turn detailed, natural-language regulatory requirements into concrete technical designs. Privacy is often conflated with security, handled “by policy” (settings and documentation) rather than “by architecture”, and engineered in an ad-hoc, project-specific way. The literature offers privacy-friendly reuse approaches, but to our knowledge none provides a systematic and operational link between legal texts, privacy requirements and their implementation in reusable software components.

Research question

How can software engineering be effectively assisted to address privacy protection from the earliest design phases?

Contributions

The thesis proposes an MDE-based framework that progressively turns a privacy regulation into privacy-aware applications. Its contribution is threefold.

1

A privacy-by-design framework

PRIAM defines the stages, activities, artefacts and roles for integrating privacy from the early design phases onward, going from the semi-formal representation of legal concepts, to requirements, to technical solutions, with tool support.

2

The PRIAM metamodel for the GDPR

An actionable metamodel organised in seven packages (Actors, Data, Processings, Consents, Rights, Breaches, Documents) with OCL constraints. A derived DSL annotates personal data and processing activities and generates generic and application-specific artefacts: GDPR user stories, a privacy-enforcement database schema and a tailored backlog.

3

PRIAM-MSA, a privacy microservice architecture

A modular architecture that decouples privacy enforcement from application logic, with an identity, authentication and authorisation service (OAuth2) and a front-end for users to exercise their rights, integrated through standard protocols and APIs with minimal intrusion.

+

Dynamic consent management

A consent manager based on ABAC that authorises each processing per user and per purpose, with a history of grants and withdrawals with timestamps, useful to end users as well as for audits.
