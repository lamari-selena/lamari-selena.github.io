PhD Thesis

Joint PhD in Computer Science (Software Engineering), Université de Montpellier (France) and Université de Blida 1 (Algeria). Defence: October 2026.

Title

Helping integrate privacy concerns into software: a model-driven engineering framework for GDPR-compliant software development.

Thesis manuscript — available after the defence Defence slides — available after the defence

Context

Software systems are increasingly personalised for their users, and the collection and exploitation of personal data has reached unprecedented levels. This raises a critical issue for the protection of users' privacy. Complying with data-protection regulations such as the European General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) has become a legal necessity.

Problem

Despite these legal obligations, there is still no universally accepted method to integrate privacy management into applications. Developers are left with regulatory texts that are declarative, while what they need are actionable means to build privacy into the software, including existing applications.

Approach and contributions

The thesis proposes a tool-supported software engineering approach that integrates personal-data protection features into applications and goes beyond a purely declarative description of the regulation. Its main elements are:

PRIAM metamodel

PRIAM (Privacy Assessment Method) models the GDPR by capturing the concepts described in the regulation. It is the foundation of all the other artefacts.

Generic and tailored artefacts

Generic user stories and a database schema derived from the metamodel, adaptable to any application that must account for privacy, plus a domain-specific language (DSL) and user stories tailored to the specific application.

Privacy-rights database

A database that stores both application-specific and user-specific data required to implement users' privacy rights.

Non-intrusive protocol

An externalised access-control (authentication and authorisation) and consent-management protocol based on the ABAC paradigm and the sidecar architecture pattern, so as to impact the provider's application as little as possible.
